| 2026-10-11 | M1 defaults where the spec is silent. Dash: goes in the movement direction, or towards the cursor when no movement key is held; the direction locks at the start; the 1.5 s cooldown counts from the start of the dash; it needs a fresh keypress and cannot leave the arena. Aim: uses the pointer's world position and keeps the last direction when the cursor is on the player. Swarmers: spawn in small groups just inside a random arena edge at a steady interval, never within a tunable minimum distance of the player, with no escalation. Feedback: the player blinks during post-hit invulnerability. Restart: R, with the prompt "You died. Press R to restart." | The spec and plan leave these details open; the defaults are the simplest predictable behaviour and stay open to playtest feedback. Escalation, knockback and screen shake belong to M2 | M1 executor prompt; plan.md, "Gameplay contract" |
| 2026-10-11 | Visual direction: 16-bit-inspired pixel art for characters, enemies, environments and UI, with modern magical effects for spells, projectiles, lighting and elemental interactions; an original identity inspired by games such as Wizard of Legend, without copying them. Supersedes the neon-geometric direction in plan.md. Current code-drawn shapes are temporary placeholders. | Product-owner decision | Product owners; plan.md, "Visual identity, audio and licensing" (amended) |
| 2026-10-11 | M2 defaults where the plan is silent. Level-ups: a brief "Level N" notice, the bar resets and leftover XP carries over; play does not pause and no reward is given. This is temporary until M3 introduces the approved pause-and-offer interaction. Spawn timeline: each entry in `src/data/spawns.json` is a complete snapshot of what spawns from that time on; the last entry continues unchanged; an enemy type spawns the moment it joins. Crystals: attracted inside the pickup range, collected on contact, never expire; at the crystal cap a new drop adds its value to the nearest crystal so no XP is lost. Feedback: enemies flash and are knocked back by projectiles, scaled by a per-enemy resistance; the player is not knocked back; the screen shakes only when the player takes damage, and an intensity of 0 turns it off. No elites, Ember, Frostback, mini-boss or boss. The HUD shows the XP bar, level and run time. Restart resets the timeline, clock, XP, level, crystals and enemies. | The plan leaves these details open; the defaults are the simplest behaviour that makes the horde and XP loop playable before upgrades exist, and stay open to playtest feedback | M2 executor prompt; plan.md, "Gameplay contract" |
| 2026-10-11 | Playtest balance: brutes from 1:15 (more frequent from 2:00); brute XP 2; XP crystals are coloured and sized by value (1 XP small cyan, 2+ XP larger purple), configured as tiers in tuning.json so later elites and bosses fit. | Product-owner playtest feedback; the plan.md values are starting candidates | PR #3 playtest |
| 2026-10-11 | Stat-boost cards (fire rate, damage, move speed, max health, pickup range) move from M4 to M3 so every level-up offers three real cards while only Fire and Frost exist. M4 keeps Split, Homing, Every Second, On Dash, Ember and Frostback. | Product-owner decision | M3 executor prompt; plan.md, "Milestones" |
| 2026-10-11 | M3 defaults where the spec and plan are silent. Offers: 3 different cards plus Skip; an installed part is never offered. While the payload slot is empty an offer is Fire, Frost and one random stat boost (the first-offer rule; it holds for every offer until a payload is installed, so the offer depends only on the loadout). After that an offer holds the payload that is not installed plus two different boosts. Cards that are not guaranteed come from a seeded generator, and an offer is built when it opens, so the second of two level-ups reflects the first choice. Stat boosts: no slot, last for the run, stack additively with no cap; starting amounts +10% fire rate, +10% damage (direct damage only; it multiplies with the payload and leaves burn alone), +8% move speed, +20 max health (current health rises with it), +25% pickup range. Payloads: Fire is x1.15 direct damage and a burn of 12 damage over 3 s in ticks every 0.5 s; Frost is x0.85 direct damage and a 40% slow for 2 s. Burn and slow refresh instead of stacking, and a refresh keeps the burn's progress towards its next tick. Every enemy death, by shot or by burn, goes through one path that drops XP once. Skip restores 10 health. Input: click or 1-3 to choose, the button or X to skip, and only presses that start after the offer opens count. Pause: enemies, shots, spawning, the run clock and every timer stop during an offer and do not catch up; a dash key held through it does not dash on resume. R is unchanged. No element resistance. Elements use placeholder colours and code-drawn effects. | The spec and plan leave these details open; the defaults are the simplest behaviour that makes the elemental acceptance test playable, and stay open to playtest feedback | M3 executor prompt; plan.md, "Gameplay contract" |
| 2026-10-11 | M3 playtest amendment, elemental balance: Fire deals 125% direct damage and Frost 110% (the plain shot is 100%). No payload may deal less direct damage than the plain shot; the parts file is validated for it. Burn (12 over 3 s) and slow (40% for 2 s) are unchanged, and boosts stack as before. This supersedes Fire +15% and Frost "lower direct damage" in the plan.md rules table and the x1.15 and x0.85 values in the M3 defaults row above. | Product-owner playtest: Frost felt like a downgrade and the pair was unsatisfying; both payloads should feel like upgrades with different strengths | M3 playtest; plan.md, "Gameplay contract" |
| 2026-10-11 | M3 playtest amendment, upgrade comparisons: each card compares the current build with the build it would give, property by property. A gain is green with an up arrow, a loss is red with a down arrow, an unchanged property of the same slot is neutral with an equals sign, so colour is never the only signal. Direct damage is shown as a percentage of the plain, unboosted shot, so payloads and damage boosts read on one scale. Whether a change is a gain is decided per property (higher or lower is better), not by the number going up. The comparison is computed from the loadout and stats, and new part types add properties without changing it. | Product-owner playtest: the earlier before/after list did not make gains and losses clear | M3 playtest; spec.md, "Slots, progression and replacement" |
| 2026-10-11 | M3 playtest amendment, world and camera: the single-screen arena becomes a finite world of 3840 by 2160 pixels (three views each way, tunable) seen through the unchanged 1280 by 720 view. The camera keeps the player centred and stops at the world's edges, so near an edge the player moves off centre and can reach the edge of the screen; the player and the dash are confined by the same world size. It is not an infinite world. The run starts in the middle. HUD and menus stay fixed to the screen; aiming converts the cursor from screen to world each frame. Enemies spawn just out of sight beyond a random side of the view and inside the world: against a boundary only the open sides are used (three, or two in a corner), still never within the minimum distance of the player. Enemies are never moved or removed because the camera moved. Shots are removed 64 pixels past the edge of the view or at the world's edge. The ground is a temporary grid with a marked strip and border at the world's edge. This supersedes the spawn rule in the M1 defaults row ("just inside a random arena edge"). | Product-owner playtest: a fixed screen did not match the intended survivor-roguelite experience | M3 playtest; plan.md, "Gameplay contract" |
| 2026-10-11 | M3 playtest amendment, enemy tuning (values to be re-tested). Swarmer: speed 190 to 170 and size 26 to 30. Shots: drawn size 10 to 12 and a hit radius of 8 instead of 5, so a shot connects from a little wider than it looks. Together a shot reaches a swarmer from about 20 pixels instead of 15.4, and swarmers are about 10% slower. Brute: speed 70 to 95, size 52 to 64 and knockback resistance 0.75 to 0.9, so it closes distance, takes up more room and can no longer be held back by shooting it; its health (60) and contact damage (25) are unchanged. No new enemy types, area damage or M4 mechanics. | Product-owner playtest: swarmers were disproportionately hard to hit and brutes too easy to avoid; changes are conservative and data-driven so the owners can judge them in another playtest | M3 playtest; plan.md, "Enemies for the initial complete run" |
